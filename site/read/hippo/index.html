DK 38 B 1

Available translations: English · German

τὰ γὰρ ὕδατα πινόμενα πάντα ἐκ τῆς θαλάσσης ἐστίν· οὐ γὰρ δή που, 〈εἰ〉 τὰ φρέατα βαθύτερα ἦν, θαλασσά ἐστιν ἐξ ἧς πίνομεν· οὕτω γὰρ οὐκ 〈ἂν〉 ἐκ τῆς θαλάσσης τὸ ὕδωρ εἴη, ἀλλ’ ἄλλοθέν ποθεν. νῦν δὲ ἡ θάλασσα βαθυτέρα ἐστὶ τῶν ὑδάτων. ὅσα οὖν καθύπερθεν τῆς θαλάσσης ἐστί, πάντα ἀπ’ αὐτῆς ἐστίν.

The waters we drink are all from the sea; for if wells were deeper than the sea, then it would not, doubtless, be from the sea that we drink, for then the water would not be from the sea, but from some other source. But as it is, the sea is deeper than the waters, so all the waters that are above the sea come from it.

John Burnet · Early Greek Philosophy, second edition (London: Adam and Charles Black, 1908), p. 407

DK 38 B 3

Available translations: English · German

πάντων αὐτὸν ἐπὶ τῆι μνήμηι θαυμασάντων ὁ Κύνουλκος ἔφη ‘ πουλυμαθημοσύνης τῆς οὐ κενεώτερον οὐδέν ‘ *** Ἵππων ἔφη ὁ ἄθεος.

and when all were marvelling at his memory, Cynulcus said—

Your multifarious learning I do wonder at—
Though there is not a thing more vain and useless,

says Hippon the Atheist.

DK 38 B 4LATIN WITNESS

Available translations: German

longe aliud anima, aliud corpus est, quae corpore et torpente viget et caeco videt et mortuo vivit.

Ganz etwas anderes ist Leib und Seele. Denn diese regt sich, wenn jener erstarrt, sieht, wenn jener erblindet, lebt, wenn jener gestorben ist.

German · Hermann Diels · Die Fragmente der Vorsokratiker, 2. Auflage, Band I (1906)
