About the reader

The aim is to bring the whole Presocratic corpus within reach: original texts, published translations and clear references to their sources.

English is the main language of the reader. Many researchers also read German, French, Italian or Spanish, and a translation in one of these languages can make a fragment accessible when English is unavailable. Where more than one translation is included, you can choose which to read.

The present edition draws from the B fragments in Diels–Kranz and the unlettered texts of chapters 89–90. Each included entry has a translation published before 1950 whose source and reuse status have been checked. A testimonia and C imitations are not yet included. The coverage page lists the current contents and omitted references by author. Further historical sources may fill these gaps, as may new translations prepared for the project, with named translators and editorial review.

Two ways to read

Reading collection is the main reader. It gathers the included fragments under their DK references, using several historical editions of the original texts and translations. The source of each passage is given beside it.

Diels 1922 is a smaller selection of translated source passages from that edition. It preserves the edition’s wording, quotation boundaries and numbering. These passages overlap the main collection; they are not additional fragments, and this is not the complete 1922 edition. Its fragment numbers should not be assumed to match the later DK references.

A witness is an ancient passage that preserves or quotes a Presocratic text, sometimes with surrounding discussion. The year 1922 identifies the edition of the original text, not the date of every translation. In either collection, the translator and edition are credited beside the passage.

Original texts

Each passage identifies its edition or transcription. The notes explain differences in wording, quotation boundaries and questions of authorship or transmission.

Other digital editions

Published translations

Each translation is credited to its translator and edition. English is shown first where available, followed by German, French, Italian and Spanish. Entries without a translation suitable for inclusion are listed in the gap register.

John Burnet’s Early Greek Philosophy (1908; 1920 where specified) and Arthur Fairbanks’s The First Philosophers of Greece (1898) supply much of the English text. The other translators are listed below and credited beside their passages. Diels’s German selections come from his 1906 and 1922 editions, not later Kranz revisions.

For Parmenides, Burnet is the default English translation for B1–17 and B19. B18 survives in Latin and uses Fairbanks, since Burnet omitted it. Supplementary entries name their own sources.

The pre-1950 cutoff is an editorial choice, not a rule of copyright law. Each translation has been reviewed separately for reuse. Later translations are outside this edition, even where permission is available.

Translation register

The register links each translation to its source and to the fragments where it appears. More than one reference may point to the same passage, and an entry may offer several languages.
